Panchkula / Chandigarh Tricity: land, access and what is worth holding

Panchkula land investment begins with geography. Panchkula is the Haryana third of the Chandigarh Tricity, at the foot of the Shivaliks, on the road to Himachal. It has a planned city's order, a hill gateway's appeal, and some of the strictest land rules in the region.

KM 018 · Access

Access today.

The Tricity is well connected by air, rail and road. Its next changes are about moving around the region more quickly, and up into the hills.

For land, the most important access is the one that already exists: Panchkula is the last flat city before the hills. Families from the plains pass through it every season.

The Tricity's metro has been planned and re-planned for years. We count it only when it is funded and building.

KM 036 · Permission

Permission in Haryana and around Chandigarh.

Panchkula's urban land is largely developed and allotted by the Haryana Shahari Vikas Pradhikaran. Outside its sectors, land use is governed by development plans for the Panchkula and Kalka–Pinjore areas, and a change of land use is needed for other purposes.

Around Chandigarh, a periphery law has long limited building in a belt around the city. Its effect varies by location, and it should be checked parcel by parcel.

In the Shivalik and Morni hills, forest and land preservation rules are strict, and an eco-sensitive zone surrounds the Sukhna wildlife sanctuary. Much of the land that looks most beautiful is the land on which least can be built.

KM 054 · Purpose

Purpose fit.

  • Light stays: a strong fit. Panchkula and Pinjore sit on the road to the hills, with gardens, forts and foothills nearby.
  • Gathering grounds: a strong fit for the Tricity, which has a large, settled population and limited open land within city limits.
  • Skill and sport grounds: a good fit, especially equestrian and outdoor disciplines suited to the foothills.
  • Working landscapes: suited to orchard and nursery land around Pinjore and Raipur Rani.

DLF has long been active here, with a large township in Panchkula. Its presence is a sign that the Tricity draws families as well as institutions.

The order of the Tricity is part of its appeal. Visitors come for clean streets, quiet sectors and the hills beyond. A purpose that matches that order, rather than fighting it, sits more easily with neighbours and with the authorities who regulate it.

KM 072 · Reading

The three levers, in the Tricity.

Access to the Tricity is long established. What is changing is access through it, into the hills, which brings more travellers past Panchkula every year.

Permission is the strictest lever here. Forest, land preservation, eco-sensitive and periphery rules overlap, and they decide more than any road does.

Purpose suits Panchkula well, on the right land. Its position as a gateway gives stays and grounds a natural audience.

KM 090 · The holders

Who holds land here.

Land around Panchkula is held by long-settled families in Pinjore and Kalka, by village families in the Morni hills and Raipur Rani, and by professionals and retired officers who chose the Tricity for its order. Many of the latter bought land for a future home, and now hold it without a plan.

The Morni hills

Morni is Haryana's own hill country, a short drive from Panchkula. It has quiet, forest and views, and strict rules on what can be built. Light, careful purposes on clearly permitted land are the only kind we would design there.

KM 108 · Caution

Where we'd be cautious.

We are cautious of any land in the Shivaliks without a clear legal reading on forest, land preservation and eco-sensitive zone rules. We are cautious of periphery restrictions near Chandigarh.

We are cautious, too, of the seasonal streams, called choes, that cross the foothills. They can be dry for years and dangerous in one night. Parcels on their banks need careful siting.

KM 126 · Signals

What would change our view.

We would read the Tricity differently if any of these moved.

  • A funded and approved plan for Tricity metro or rapid transit.
  • The Zirakpur bypass and the four-laning towards Shimla completed.
  • Any change to periphery or eco-sensitive zone boundaries.
  • New homestay and farm tourism rules in Haryana.

The last of these matters most for purpose. Haryana's long-promised farm tourism and homestay policy could make light stays near Panchkula far simpler to register and run. Until it is approved, we design under the schemes that exist today.

KM 144 · Before you buy

Questions before you buy.

  1. Is any part of the parcel forest, land preservation or eco-sensitive zone land?
  2. Does the periphery law apply to this location?
  3. Which development plan governs it, and which zone is it in?
  4. Does a seasonal stream cross it, or run along its edge?
  5. Is the approach road usable in the monsoon?
