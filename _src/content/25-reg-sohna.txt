## Access today. {Access}

Sohna's access changed sharply in the last few years. Two road projects brought it within easy reach of both Gurugram and the Delhi–Mumbai Expressway.

Developers have followed the roads. Signature Global bought about ten acres in Sohna in mid-2025, and reported collaboration agreements and further purchases of about 25 acres there in August 2026. {tv:Signature Global Sohna land: ~9.96 acres (Jun–Jul 2025); collaboration ~11.9 acres plus subsidiary ~13.7 acres (Aug 2026)} [Outlook Business](https://www.outlookbusiness.com/corporate/signature-global-buys-2-land-parcels-in-gurugram-totalling-10-acres-for-housing-projects) reported the earlier purchase.

That pattern, roads first and developers second, is the clearest Access signal in the region. It also means land close to the main roads is no longer early.

## Permission in Haryana. {Permission}

Sohna has its own development plan, administered by the Department of Town and Country Planning. {tv:Sohna Development Plan 2031} Urbanisable sectors are licensed for colonies. The agricultural zone around them needs a change of land use for any other purpose.

The Aravalli foothills on Sohna's edges carry forest, land preservation and conservation-zone rules. {tv:Aravalli restrictions around Sohna: PLPA notifications and NCR Natural Conservation Zone} These rules decide more about Sohna's land than any road. A parcel half in and half out of a restricted area is two different assets.

Water is a planning question here as much as a natural one. Permissions for hospitality and gathering uses look closely at water, waste and access.

## Purpose fit. {Purpose}

- **Light stays:** a strong fit. Sohna offers quiet and hills within an hour of a very large city, which is what light stays sell.
- **Gathering grounds:** a strong fit, for families and companies leaving Gurugram for a day.
- **Skill and sport grounds:** a strong fit. Equestrian, archery and outdoor disciplines suit the setting and the catchment.
- **Working landscapes:** suited to land that is held for later, especially where water allows.

Sohna is one of the few places where all four purposes can work. That makes the choice of purpose, and of parcel, more important, not less.

The choice between these purposes should follow the parcel, not fashion. A flat parcel near the main road suits grounds and gatherings. A quieter parcel against the hills, clear of restrictions, suits a few rooms. Water decides how many.

## The three levers, in Sohna. {Reading}

Access is new in Sohna, and largely priced close to the main roads. A few kilometres away, along narrower roads into the foothills, it is priced much less fully.

Permission is decisive here, because of the Aravalli lines and the sector boundaries. Two parcels on the same road can have entirely different futures.

Purpose is where Sohna has the most to give. Its setting suits stays, grounds and gatherings better than almost anywhere so close to a large city.

## Who holds land here. {The holders}

Land around Sohna is held by village families in the foothills, by city residents who bought weekend land years ago, and by developers building land banks. Many foothill parcels were bought informally by city buyers long ago. Their records and their current use do not always match the rules.

For a buyer, that history is a reason for patient diligence. For a holder, it is a reason to regularise before trying to sell or develop.

### Water, on its own line

Groundwater in the Aravalli foothills is uneven, and parts of the area are notified for regulated extraction. {tv:Groundwater notified or over-exploited blocks around Sohna (CGWA / Haryana Water Resources Authority)} A purpose that needs water for guests, gardens and animals must be designed around a reliable source, rainwater harvesting and careful reuse.

## Where we'd be cautious. {Caution}

We are cautious of any parcel in or near the Aravalli without a clear legal reading. We are cautious of water: some parcels cannot support the purpose their buyers imagine.

We are cautious, too, of land priced on developer activity a few kilometres away. Developers buy where licences and sector roads make sense for them. A farm parcel down a narrow approach does not inherit their logic.

## What would change our view. {Signals}

We would read Sohna differently if any of these moved.

- Sector roads and services actually built under the Sohna development plan.
- Any revision of Aravalli or conservation-zone boundaries.
- The water position, and any new rules on extraction.
- Where developers buy next, and whether they buy outright or collaborate.

The last of these is the quiet signal. Developers' choices show where they expect licences to follow.

## Questions before you buy. {Before you buy}

1. Is any part of the parcel within an Aravalli, forest or conservation restriction?
2. Is it in an urbanisable sector, or in the agricultural zone?
3. What is the water source, and what does it support?
4. How wide is the approach road, by measurement, not by description?
5. What purpose do you intend, and does the zone permit it?
