## Why developers are the earliest signal. {The essay}

By the time a road opens, the market has priced it. By the time a project launches, the land under it was bought years earlier. The people who bought it were reading the same signals we read, with more money at stake.

That makes developers' land moves worth following. When a developer buys or signs a collaboration in a new area, it is telling you what it expects: that access will arrive, that permission is obtainable, and that buyers will follow.

Developers can be wrong. Some land banks have waited decades. Some corridors never delivered. But a developer's money, committed in public filings and registered deeds, is a better signal than an advertisement.

For a landowner, there is a second reason to read them. A developer active near your land may one day approach you. Knowing how it buys, whether outright, by collaboration or through authority auctions, is the start of a fair negotiation.

## How to read these notes. {Method}

Each note follows the same discipline. Facts come from public sources: annual reports, stock-exchange filings, regulators' portals, authority records and reputable reporting. Each is dated, and each is checked before it is relied upon.

We read every developer through the same three levers we use for land: Access, Permission and Purpose. We never discuss a developer's projects as products, never give prices, and never recommend buying anything from anyone.

These notes are general and educational. They are written for landowners and families holding land, not for apartment buyers.
