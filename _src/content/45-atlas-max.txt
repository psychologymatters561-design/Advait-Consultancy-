## Where Max Estates has been active. {Where}

Max Estates' activity began with commercial buildings in Noida and Delhi, held for rent, and has extended into residential development in Noida and Gurugram. {tv:Max Estates commercial assets in Noida and Delhi; residential in Noida Sector 128 and Gurugram} In Noida it has taken part in the authority's land auctions. {tv:Max Estates Noida Authority auction participation}

In Gurugram, in September 2025, it acquired the whole of a company that held a licence and development rights over 7.25 acres on Golf Course Extension Road, as reported at the time. The structure is the point of interest here: the company, with its licence, was bought rather than the land alone.

## What Max Estates' moves signal. {Signal}

**Access.** Max Estates has favoured locations where access is already established: city sectors with roads, metro and offices in place. Its presence signals a mature area, not an early one.

**Permission.** Buying a company that already holds a licence shows plainly where value sits. A licence takes years to obtain, and it carries much of a parcel's developed value. A landowner whose land is licensed, or close to it, holds something worth considerably more than farmland.

**Purpose.** A developer that holds commercial buildings for rent thinks about land as something that earns, not only something that sells. That is close to how we ask families to think about their own land.

## If you own land where Max Estates is active. {Landowners}

The most useful lesson for a landowner here is about structure. Land can change hands as land, as a share of development, or inside a company that owns it and its licence. Each has different consequences.

### Selling land, or selling a company

Selling land is the familiar route: a sale deed, stamp duty, and capital gains on the land. Selling a company that holds the land and its licence is a different transaction. Liabilities, tax treatment and warranties all differ, and the buyer will ask for many assurances about the company's past. {tv:Share sale vs land sale: stamp duty, tax and liability differences; confirm with tax counsel} It is a route for families who have already obtained a licence, and it needs lawyers and tax counsel from the first day.

### Licensed land is a different asset

If your land holds a licence, or could obtain one, value it as licensed land, not as farmland with an approval attached. Developers will. The family should know that number before any conversation begins.

### Joint development, sale, or hold and design

The usual three paths still apply. A collaboration shares developed value. A sale, of land or of a company, gives certainty. Holding with a purpose keeps the land earning in the family's hands.

### Questions to ask any developer

1. Do you want to buy the land, share in its development, or buy the company that holds it?
2. Why that structure, and who benefits from it?
3. What warranties and indemnities would you ask of us, and for how long?
4. How have you valued the licence, separately from the land?
5. What happens to our agreement if the licence conditions change?

These are general questions that apply to any developer. They are not a comment on Max Estates' agreements, which we have not reviewed.

## Rental thinking for families. {Holding to earn}

Max Estates holds some of its buildings and rents them, rather than selling everything it builds. That is a way of thinking about property that families with land can borrow.

A family does not need to sell land to earn from it. It can lease part of it to an operator who runs a ground, a stay or a working farm, under a written agreement that keeps the land in the family's hands. The rent is modest compared with a sale. It is also repeatable, and it leaves the land, and the decision, with the family.

This is the centre of our [value design](/services/value-design/) work: land that earns while the corridor matures.

### Licence conditions

A licence comes with conditions, and some carry deadlines. If a family holds licensed land, or a company that does, those conditions affect what the land is worth and how long the family can wait. Read them with your lawyers before any negotiation.

## If you are placing capital nearby. {Capital}

We don't advise on buying apartments, including theirs. What an established developer's presence does tell you about nearby land is that the area's access and permission are largely settled. Value there is in use, design and patience, rather than in waiting for the next road.
