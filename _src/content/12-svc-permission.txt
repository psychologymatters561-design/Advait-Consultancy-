## When families call us about this. {The problem first}

Permission problems arrive in two forms. Either nothing has been applied for, or something was applied for years ago and has not moved.

- The family wants to use agricultural land for something else, and does not know if it is allowed.
- A change of land use application has been pending for years, with no clear reason.
- A purpose has been designed, but nobody has checked whether the zone permits it.
- Land sits in a green belt or a conservation zone, and the family has been promised otherwise.
- The family has been told an outright sale is impossible in that state.

Each of these needs a clear reading before any application. A rejected application costs time, fees and credibility with the office that decides the next one.

## How permission works, state by state. {By state}

Every state has its own law, its own office and its own culture of decision. What follows is general. It is not legal advice.

### Haryana: change of land use

In Haryana, land in a controlled area or an urbanisable zone needs a change of land use permission, known as CLU, before it is used for a non-agricultural purpose. The Department of Town and Country Planning grants it. {tv:Haryana CLU process: DTCP under the 1963 Controlled Areas Act} The purpose must be permissible in that zone of the development plan.

The process is a sequence. Check the zone. Check the approach road and site norms. Prepare the application and drawings. Pay the scrutiny and conversion charges. Answer the objections. Each step can be done well or badly, and the difference shows up in months.

Many families ask about farmhouses near Gurugram. Haryana's development plans permit farmhouses in the agricultural zone under specific conditions on area, coverage and access. {tv:Haryana farmhouse norms in agricultural zone of development plans; minimum area and coverage} Those conditions change, and we read the current ones before anyone builds.

### Uttar Pradesh: Section 80

In Uttar Pradesh, Section 80 of the UP Revenue Code, 2006 allows a declaration that agricultural land is used for a non-agricultural purpose. {tv:UP Revenue Code 2006 s.80 declaration process and fee} It is made through the revenue administration and changes how the land is recorded and taxed.

Inside a notified development authority's area, such as the Yamuna Expressway Industrial Development Authority, the authority's master plan and allotment terms decide instead. Land there is often held on lease from the authority, which changes the arithmetic entirely.

### Himachal Pradesh: Section 118

In Himachal Pradesh, Section 118 of the HP Tenancy and Land Reforms Act, 1972 restricts the transfer of land to people who are not agriculturists of the state, without government permission. An amendment bill was sent to a Select Committee of the Assembly in December 2025. {tv:HP s.118 amendment bill referred to Select Committee, Dec 2025; check current status} Where purchase is restricted, a long lease or a joint venture with a landowning family is often the honest structure.

### Delhi: caution first

Agricultural land in Delhi's villages is governed by the Delhi Land Reforms Act, 1954, which restricts both its division and its use for other purposes. {tv:Delhi Land Reforms Act 1954 ss.33 and 81} Families holding such land should take legal advice before any change of use.

## Why applications stall. {Stalled files}

Most stalled applications share a cause that was visible on the first day. The purpose did not fit the zone. The approach road was narrower than the norm. A title question was left for later.

Some stall for reasons outside the family's control: a revised development plan, a new policy, a change of officer. Those need patience and a clean file, not pressure.

When a family brings us a stalled file, we read it from the beginning. Sometimes the right move is to answer the objections properly. Sometimes it is to withdraw and apply again, for a purpose the land can actually carry.

## Questions to ask before you apply. {Before you apply}

1. Which development plan governs this land, and which zone is it in?
2. Is the purpose I want permitted in that zone, as written today?
3. Does the land meet the road-width and site norms for that purpose?
4. What will the permission cost in charges, before any building?
5. What happens to the value if the permission is refused?
6. Is there a conservation, forest or green-belt rule over any part of it?
7. Who is preparing the application, and who is accountable for it?
